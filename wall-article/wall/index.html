The wall is your best hitting partner

How to turn 20 minutes on a wall into real match sharpness.

By Aiden · 4 min read

It never misses. It never cancels. It's free. And it's probably the most underrated training tool in tennis.

When I came back after three years away from the game, I didn't start with lessons or matches. I started on a wall. No expectations. Just me, a ball, and one goal: keep my eyes on it.

That's what the wall really trains: focus. Every ball comes back faster than you expect, so your mind can't wander. You either stay locked in or the rally ends.

The calibration exercise

Think of this drill as calibrating your contact. Before you work on anything else, you teach your body to find the center of the strings every time.

  1. Watch the contactWatch the ball hit your strings, then count "one Mississippi" in your head before you look up to see where it went. Don't follow the ball with your eyes or peek at the target while you're hitting. Contact first, then look. You may need to shorten the count when you're standing close.
  2. Set up a targetA sticky note, a strip of masking tape, anything you can come back to every session. If it's a private wall, don't damage it. Don't become a vandal.
  3. Start close, control before powerStand close to the wall and alternate forehand, backhand. Keep your strokes tiny and stay as loose as you can. All you're focused on is centering the ball on the strings. This isn't about technique. Hit the target 10 times in a row before you back up.
  4. Back up five stepsOnce you've earned it, take about five steps back and keep going, one shot at a time.
  5. Play switch for backhandsIf your backhand needs work, try hitting a one-handed forehand with your other hand on that side. It sounds strange, and it works.
  6. Work in 5-minute blocksIf you lose focus or get tired, take a break and come back for another 5 minutes.

Don't back up because you're bored. Back up because you earned it.

This is the part most people skip. They get mentally tired, stand further back, and start swinging. Take the time to build your control from the start. It's what everything else is built on.

Any wall works

Don't get picky about the wall. A little texture makes the bounce less predictable, which forces your footwork to be sharper. A brick wall is arguably better than a perfectly flat one.

Record yourself

Prop your phone up and film your sessions. It's the only way to actually see how much your contact and control are improving from week to week.

A 20-minute weekday session

0:00–5:00Close to the wall. Tiny strokes, alternating sides, watching contact.
5:00–10:00Keep going until you hit the target 10 in a row. Take a break if you need it.
10:00–15:00Back up five steps, one shot at a time. Play switch if you're working on backhands.
15:00–20:00Record attempt. How many in a row can you hit the target?

Write your record down. Beat it next week.

If you've only got 20 minutes on a weekday, the wall is how you stay sharp for the weekend.
